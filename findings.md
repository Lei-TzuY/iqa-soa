## Research Findings
- Workspace initially contains `2026-final.pdf` and `FSE_2027_IQA_SOA_Draft_v0.1.docx`.
- The 44-page 2026 proposal is titled “Research on User-Centric LLM-Driven Autonomous Evolution System for Human-Cyber-Physical Intelligent Services.”
- The core reframes an LLM as a constrained semantic compiler, not the ultimate decision-maker, with an engineering middle layer providing runtime-controllable governance.
- Four research pillars are: natural-language intent to executable QA-XML system-level quality constraints; pluggable IQA-SOA runtime governance; lifecycle QA-IUM evidence graph; and safety-aware multi-objective optimization across quality, cost, and risk.
- Named validation modes in the abstract are adversarial red-teaming, fault injection, log replay, formal verification, and convergence analysis.
- Named outcome measures in the abstract include constraint satisfaction rate, risk interception rate, false rejection rate, cost reduction ratio, governance-overhead bounds, overall stability, Pareto improvement, and long-term evolutionary stability under explicit safety boundaries.
- The paper's core hypothesis is that dynamic intent-to-constraint mapping plus full-path runtime monitoring and multi-objective orchestration can bound normal/abnormal service behavior and find a dynamic optimum while preserving a user-satisfaction floor. It alternately names the trade space quality–cost–efficiency and quality–cost–risk; the research implementation should preserve the latter safety-aware form and note the wording variation.
- Two authoritative scientific questions are: (1) how subjective, changing natural-language intent can be dynamically mapped to executable system constraints that filter hallucination and privilege-escalation risk; and (2) how evidence-chain-based runtime multi-objective optimization can balance quality, cost, and safety risk under resource limits and environmental drift.
- The key conceptual move is layered governance: the LLM performs intent understanding/candidate strategy generation as a controlled semantic compiler; deterministic middleware validates, trims, monitors, audits, circuit-breaks, and rolls back candidates.
- The proposal explicitly distinguishes this from classic MAPE-K (assumes a known/deterministically controllable model) and static goal-model requirements engineering (not suited to dynamic stochastic generations).
- The lifecycle loop is “monitor–feedback–optimize,” with runtime evidence driving incremental policy evolution; user-defined satisfaction, budget/compliance, and risk limits retain final authority.
- UC-SCEM (User-Centric Service Computing Evaluation Model) is the quantitative anchor: a three-layer evaluation framework spanning task-logic effectiveness, non-deterministic delivery quality, and global runtime/governance compliance, conditioned on the user-preference × runtime-context × domain-risk triple.
- QA-XML expands to Quality Assurance eXtensible Markup Language. It is the typed, formally defined executable intermediate representation between ambiguous intent and precise checks—not merely configuration. Its five orthogonal constraint/execution dimensions are input, inference, tool, output, and disposition/response.
- QA-UML is the visual collaboration notation paired with QA-XML so business experts, compliance auditors, and engineers can review the same semantics.
- The QA-XML compiler pipeline is: semantic structured extraction → AST construction → formal verification → probe code generation. SMT-based checks detect conflicting constraints and compile-time feasibility; semantic-boundary constraints generate adversarial test cases.
- IQA-SOA expands to Intelligent Quality Assurance Service Oriented Architecture. Its three primary roles are Service Gateway (single governance entry), Service Decorator (non-invasive interception/probes), and QA Module (dynamic decision core). QA modules form a context-risk-aware, hot-swappable responsibility chain.
- IQA-SOA enforcement spans input injection/adversarial cleaning; inference model allowlists and context-window circuit breaking; tool/action authorization for plan–execute loops; output hallucination/evidence/sensitive-data checks; and declared degradation/circuit-break responses through standardized I/O contracts.
- QA-IUM expands to Quality Assurance Integrated Unified Model (also punctuated “Quality Assurance - Integrated Unified Model”). It is a lifecycle DAG evidence graph linking natural-language requirements → QA-XML constraints → bound checkpoints/probes → runtime model/config/tool-call state → interception records → disposition actions.
- QA-IUM must support forward tracing from a requirement to deployed enforcement and backward root-cause tracing from an incident, cross-artifact consistency checks, rollback evidence, and regulator-facing audit interfaces. Proposed checks cover spec-to-probe coverage, event/threshold-to-model-config consistency, and disposition-to-policy consistency; deep hazards include configured-but-inactive rules, forged logs, and wrong attribution.
- QA-IUM's concrete research target includes evidence queries under 500 ms and accurate fault-node localization; a broader goal mentions second-level queries at million-log scale and successful evidence-driven rollback.
- The optimizer's action space includes model choice, prompt template, RAG depth K, and human-handoff threshold. It uses CBwK or Safe RL, but proposals remain candidates: IQA-SOA performs the non-bypassable hard safety adjudication.
- The Quality-Based Cost Model unifies direct compute/API cost, delay/user-loss cost, and latent privacy/privilege-risk penalties. QA-IUM feedback updates cost estimates and transition probabilities from rejected strategies and expensive incident handling.
- The explicit optimization target is Pareto improvement while maintaining at least 95% constraint satisfaction; claimed algorithmic work includes convergence analysis in non-stationary environments and user-interpretable preference intervention.
- Experimental families are comparison, ablation, red-team, and replay. Baselines are no governance, partial governance, and full IQA-SOA; optimization comparisons add no optimization, cost-greedy only, and quality-first only. Ablations individually remove injection detection, privacy protection, tool constraints, budget guard, and evidence output.
- Attack/fault scenarios include prompt injection, jailbreak templates, privilege/action escalation, privacy extraction, hallucination/factual error, deepfake traffic, poisoned knowledge bases, retrieval-latency spikes, compute-node failure, high concurrency, and context/load drift.
- The paper groups results into risk-event curves (leakage, privilege violation, factual error), availability/user-experience curves (task completion and justified refusal), and cost/latency/resource curves (per-request spend and response-peak stability). Its strongest evidence contrasts cost reduction at a fixed safety threshold and risk reduction at a fixed cost.
- The four-year staging is explicit: Y1 user–context–risk model, QA-XML v1/compiler/schema+conflict validation, basic IQA-SOA gateway with budget/privacy/permission controls, templates/samples, and a baseline report; Y2 QA-IUM store/audit export, higher-order injection/privilege modules, rollback interface; Y3 UC-SCEM, dynamic model/RAG routing, degradation/circuit breaking/human collaboration, and evidence-driven drift adaptation; Y4 cross-domain scale/transfer evaluation and standardized reusable middleware/reference implementation.
- The proposal's available implementation foundations are conventional and composable: SMT solving, Pareto optimization, MAPE-K feedback concepts, Safe RL, GNN/abstract interpretation, SOA, access-control/audit work, and LLM/medical-service experience. It does not prescribe a concrete programming language, wire protocol, database, model vendor, dataset name, sample size, random seeds, repetitions, or statistical test in the read research sections.
- The existing DOCX is a manuscript skeleton, not reusable source code. It narrows the study to runtime enforcement, defines the core separation `LLM reasoning != execution authority`, treats QA-IUM as supporting audit infrastructure, and contains `[TBD]` placeholders rather than claimed results.
- The DOCX formalizes candidate generation `P = M(u,e)`, action sequence `P={a1,...,an}`, constraints `C={c1,...,cm}`, admissibility `a_i ⊨ C`, and governance `G(a_i,C,e) → {ALLOW,BLOCK,MODIFY,ESCALATE}`. This matches the master prompt and paper's layered-governance premise.
- The DOCX identifies validity threats (construct, internal/stochasticity, sandbox external validity, benchmark bias, and model dependence) and explicitly defers repetition count `k` to pilot measurements/power considerations.

## Technical Decisions
| Decision | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| Read the full proposal before implementation | Architecture and vocabulary follow the research source of truth rather than inferred meanings from the implementation prompt. |
| Build at the workspace root | The workspace contains only research documents and planning files, so nesting another repository would add no preservation value. |
| Treat the DOCX as a manuscript skeleton, subordinate to the PDF and prompt | It contains no code and explicitly marks results/citations incomplete; its terminology is consistent with the paper but its narrowed RQ framing is not the source of truth where the master prompt is more specific. |
| Use deterministic benchmark scripts for the executable artifact, with an optional HTTP provider | Enables exact paired proposals and CI reproducibility while preserving a path to stochastic-model runs without requiring credentials. |
| Use a single `src/iqa_soa` package with agent, tools, iqa, benchmark, experiment, metrics, and evidence subpackages | Keeps the requested modular boundaries without polluting Python with generic top-level packages. |
| Define G1/partial as permission + budget guards | The proposal leaves partial governance unspecified; these are conventional, deterministic action controls and the exact subset will be declared in config/results. |
| Evaluate injection on benchmark-declared untrusted content and patterns | This is measurable and reproducible, and avoids falsely presenting a heuristic as general prompt-injection detection. |
| Store QA-IUM-compatible append-only JSONL evidence fragments rather than a graph DB | Satisfies traceability needs in the narrowed FSE artifact while respecting the explicit non-goal of a full industrial QA-IUM graph database. |
| Treat online repetitions as nested within task, using task-cluster bootstrap/sign-flip inference; suppress deterministic-fixture inference entirely | Avoids treating repeated executions of eight shared tasks as independent evidence and explicitly limits the estimand to this fixed benchmark suite. |
| Load the FULL/full-minus-one maps from strict YAML | Satisfies the prompt's data-driven ablation requirement and makes every enabled guard map inspectable and hash-bound. |
| Use a critical-only confirmation threshold in the experiment policy while deriving high-impact tool risk outside the proposer | Preserves injection/privacy mechanism isolation without allowing an LLM to downgrade the severity of outbound or destructive actions; deployments can configure the stricter high threshold. |
| Require a complete manifest Cartesian product and source/configuration hashes before analysis | Stops interrupted, stale-schema, duplicated, error-bearing, or mismatched runs from silently becoming evidence. |
| Preserve historical/pilot artifacts and mark superseded derivatives | Maintains research integrity and non-overwrite guarantees while identifying the canonical validation set. |

- Removing injection causes six fixture violations; permission, privacy, budget, and output validation each expose three; removing evidence makes all 24 ablation evidence records incomplete without fabricating a safety effect.
- Remaining scoped limitations are declared rather than hidden: heuristic regex injection detection, Python-regex configuration trust, simulated tools/latency, post-response limits for already-consumed token/cost/runtime usage, a small hand-authored benchmark, nullable unknown cost, QA-IUM-compatible JSONL rather than a graph/tamper-proof store, and no real-model generalization claim.

## Pilot-v5: canonical resource/action contract (2026-08-16)

- Diagnosis across all 12 pilot-v4 cases (`case.canonical_resources` derivation, verified against the actual YAML): 9 of 12 declare at least one multi-segment (`category/id`-style) resource -- `BEN-002`, `BUD-001`, `FAULT-002`, `KP-001`, `PI-002`, `PRIV-001`, `PRIV-002`, `PRIV-003`, `UA-001`, `UA-003` (only `BEN-001`, `PI-001`'s file resource, and the two message/shell single-segment resources are single-segment). `PRIV-001` additionally has a genuine basename collision (`public/profile.txt` vs `private/profile.txt`, both reducing to `profile.txt`) -- any naive "reconstruct the resource" rule risks silently guessing the wrong one there, which is exactly the failure mode the design must refuse to produce. - Design (`src/iqa_soa/benchmark/schema.py: BenchmarkCase.canonical_resources`, `src/iqa_soa/agent/providers.py: _resolve_canonical_resource`/`_native_action_tool`): a per-case finite resource vocabulary derived only from `environment.files`/`database`/`api_responses` keys plus literal (non-wildcard) entries in `allowed_resources`/`forbidden_resources`/`protected_resources` -- never from `ground_truth` or `scripted_actions`, so allowed and forbidden identifiers are exposed together, undifferentiated (no hint which one is "the test"). A proposal that still doesn't resolve remains a genuine `invalid_resource`/malformed-resource outcome, exactly as before. `ProviderResponse.original_resource`/`canonical_resource` preserve both the raw and resolved values in `provider_attempts` provenance, so canonicalization is never silent.
